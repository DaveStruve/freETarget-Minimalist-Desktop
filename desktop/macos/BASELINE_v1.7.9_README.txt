FIVE-SHOT STANDARD -- FIRST USE
1. Select your shooter and C55P (add it in Shooter Profiles if necessary).
2. Choose Air Pistol > Five-Shot Standard. For one five-shot magazine, choose
   1 Record Series and turn off One Sighting Series. Preparation can be disabled
   for a short session. For the full programme use 8 series, or select 6.
3. Use Test Commands and check speaker output/Voice volume before Start.
4. Press Start. Follow the phase display and LOAD / ATTENTION / START / STOP
   commands. Stay on the app's visible tab for the sequence.
5. After Stop, wait for delayed target reports and inspect the five-shot result.
   Press Next Series only when that result is ready. If reports are missing,
   committing requests confirmation before filling remaining slots with zeros.
6. In the review, tick Zero (Outside Window) only for a shot you know was early
   or late. You can clear the checkbox to restore its original score.
7. Finish Session opens the existing log/discard screen. A named shooter can
   save the result to History; Guest ends without saving as before.

TIMING / SCORING LIMITS
This is a browser-guided event, not a timing-certified competition system.
Voice playback follows the browser audio clock. Device output latency and the
physical target were not measured here. Wi-Fi/USB reports can arrive after the
actual shot, including after Stop; the browser receipt time is not a verified
shot time. Hardware timestamps are not treated as synchronized timing proof.
The app provisionally keeps the first five reports received from Attention
through the post-Stop review. It does not automatically call an after-Stop
arrival a late shot or assign it a timing zero. Early/late decisions are manual.
Reports received during loading are not scored. Additional reports beyond five
are excluded from the score and retained in the event audit. Do not start the
next string while results from the current string are still being delivered.
Interrupted strings require a restart; confirmation removes current shots from
scoring and keeps them in the interrupted-series audit. Early session logging
also stops the sequence and can commit a partially fired string with confirmed
unfired zeros. History records browser-guided timing, manual timing penalties,
unfired zeros, extra reports and the committed/interrupted-series audit.
The format follows the ISSF Sport for All 2017 Standard event sequence on a
single target. It does not implement jury procedures, malfunction adjudication,
shoot-offs or the five-target event. Skipped loading/preparation is audited.
Official source, linked from ISSF Sports for All:
https://www.issf-sports.org/rules/sports-for-all
https://backoffice.issf-sports.org/getfile.aspx?file=2017_ISSF_Rules_-_10m_Five-Shot_Air_Pistol_-_Edition.pdf&inst=290&mod=docf&pane=1
Embedded cue audio was synthesized locally with CMU Flite (SLT voice).

STATISTICS DEFINITIONS / DATA LIMITS
Score % uses earned points / (recorded shots x saved maximum): 10 for integer,
10.9 for decimal. Partial rounds use their recorded shot count, not a planned
match quota. The overview average weights by recorded shots; best and latest
refer to individual sessions. Change compares the first and latest dated
session in the current filtered period. It is descriptive, not a forecast.
Unknown scoring methods do not receive an invented maximum. They remain
accessible under Scoring > Unspecified. Older summary-only records can supply
percentages and known X counts, but not missing individual-shot statistics.
Mean radius averages each hit's distance from its own session's mean impact.
The dashboard combines those radii by hit count. Group spread is the exact
largest center-to-center distance between hits; the overview shows the median
session spread. At least two valid hits are needed for group size. Center
Offset is distance of mean impact from target center. The pooled Mean Impact
X/Y card can hide opposing session offsets, so compare Center Offset as well.
Coordinates use the saved target scale. Positive X is right, positive Y is high
in the displayed impact card. Display-circle padding is never included.
Sighting shots are not included. Misses and synthetic timeout/advance positions
are excluded from grouping; their score zeros still count in scoring metrics.
Coverage counts identify the data available for each metric. Missing values
show a dash. Score SD is population SD, a measure of score variation; a low SD
alone does not imply a high score. Different formats and shot counts still
influence comparisons; use like-for-like filters. No hold/aim trace or trigger
measurements are inferred from impact locations. Dates use the browser's local
calendar. The rolling mean is computed only from the currently visible cohort.
Research references, also linked in the History explanation:
- TargetScan: score percentage, mean radius and impact-position trends:
  https://targetshootingapp.com/wiki/Stat_Charts/
- Standard deviation definition:
  https://www.nlm.nih.gov/oet/ed/stats/02-900.html

START / UPGRADE
1. Stop the previous bridge with Ctrl+C.
2. Extract the entire ZIP into a fresh folder.
3. Windows: run START_freETarget.bat.
   macOS: run START_freETarget.command, or use: python3 freetarget_bridge.py
4. Open http://127.0.0.1:8765 in the same browser used previously and refresh.
5. Confirm v1.7.9 in the footer. Existing profiles, formats, colors and History
   remain associated with that browser/address.
Wi-Fi/TCP works with Python 3.10+ and does not require additional packages.
No firmware changes are included or required by this build.

USB SETUP (ONE TIME)
Windows: run INSTALL_USB_SUPPORT.bat, then restart START_freETarget.bat.
macOS: run INSTALL_USB_SUPPORT.command, then restart the bridge.
These install pyserial==3.5 for the same Python interpreter used by the launchers.
Internet access is needed for this installation; it is optional for Wi-Fi users.
Manual equivalent: python -m pip install -r requirements.txt
On macOS use python3 instead of python. If your Python blocks system-package
installation, create a virtual environment, install requirements there, and run
freetarget_bridge.py with that environment's Python.

Settings > Connection:
- Select Wi-Fi / TCP or USB / Serial.
- USB: Refresh ports, choose a detected device, or enter a serial port manually
  (e.g. COM3 on Windows or /dev/cu.usbserial... on macOS).
- Default serial settings: 115200 baud, 8 data bits, no parity, 1 stop bit.
  Baud rate is editable. Save, then use Connect to Target in the sidebar.
- Close Arduino Serial Monitor and other software using that port first.
- Reconnect USB automatically is enabled by default. The reader retries after
  unplug/replug. When a unique USB serial identity is available, it can follow
  that same device to a changed port name. Without one it retries the saved port.
  It will not guess between multiple devices sharing an identity.
- Disconnect / Cancel connection stops both a connection and a reconnect attempt.
- The last successful connection is remembered. Use last successful connection
  in Settings loads it for review; Save applies it to the next connection.
- Some Arduino boards/drivers reset on opening USB. This build holds DTR/RTS
  inactive before opening to reduce resets; wait for the target to initialize.
- Serial input and TCP input share the same JSON framing, malformed-comma repair
  and shot event path. Trace text is diagnostic data, not a scoring shot.
- Both Wi-Fi station and access-point connections still use the saved TCP host.
Only one transport is opened at a time. Commands use that same transport.

TARGET SETTINGS / ARDUINO TERMINAL
1. Connect to the target, then open Target Settings in the footer.
2. Choose Get Settings. Fields remain disabled until current device values arrive.
3. Edit Hardware or Sensors values, then Apply and review the confirmation.
   An active session pauses before a configuration write; resume it manually.
4. Apply waits for read-back and reports success only when values match. A
   missing response or write error is reported without automatically repeating
   the write. Get Settings again to inspect the device after an uncertain result.
5. Reconnecting invalidates the form; Get Settings again before editing.
Motor Time and Step Duration use milliseconds in the supplied firmware.
Stepper requires positive Steps and Step Duration; DC mode clears both fields.
Per-sensor offsets are separate from the unsupported global calibration offsets.
Terminal commands that can change the device request confirmation and pause
an active session. INIT resets stored configuration, including connection
settings. ECHO and VERSION are read queries. Commands are split below the
firmware input-buffer limit when applying multiple fields. Generic JSON accepts
one object and plain ASCII scalar parameters; unsupported CAL/global offsets
and unsafe firmware delimiters are rejected.
Terminal output continues independently of the General Diagnostics preference.
Clearing its console does not clear diagnostics or discard incoming shots.

Enable TCP / USB Diagnostics (default on)

  registration continue. Raw display messages are not appended while hidden.

  shot-event cursor, so clearing diagnostics cannot erase a pending shot.

Ignore Misses (default off)
- Only explicitly flagged target misses are excluded. They do not advance
  scores, count, average, target pages or stage shot quotas.
- Valid coordinate shots that score zero still count.
- Changing the setting during a round applies to subsequent target shots.
- Sessions record whether the option was used and how many record/sighting
  misses were excluded. Turning it off later does not erase that audit flag.
- Rules outline, log summary and History indicate use. Qualification, Final
  and league results are marked as modified competition results.
- Final timeouts and early-advance zero slots still count; those are stage
  penalties, not incoming target misses filtered by this option.

Show Mean Group (default off)
- A mean-impact crosshair and subtle red dashed group circle appear on the
  currently displayed target page, including preview, lightbox and History.
- At least two plotted hits are required. Misses and invalid coordinates are
  excluded; a valid zero-score impact with coordinates can still contribute.
- The crosshair is the arithmetic mean of the page's hit coordinates.
- The boundary is the smallest circle containing all plotted impact centers,
  with padding to enclose the visible markers and selection halo. It is
  separate from the mean crosshair. Two or three outer impacts determine the
  circle; other impacts can be inside. This is a visual enclosing boundary,
  not a confidence interval or a physical group-diameter measurement.
- Works with pistol/rifle, zoom and page navigation. It does not alter scoring.
All General settings are remembered between launches.
